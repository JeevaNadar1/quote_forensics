# Domain: BOQ, construction, fit-out, MEP

Load when quotes are bills of quantities, civil/interior/MEP tenders, or site-based work.

---

## What differs here

1. **Quantities are the buyer's, rates are the vendor's.** A gap in a BOQ tender is almost
   always a rate story, not a quantity story. If quantities differ, that is an intake
   defect — report it before analysing rates.
2. **Preliminaries and P&OH** are quoted separately or loaded into rates. Two quotes are
   not comparable until you know which. A quote with low rates and high prelims can beat a
   quote with the opposite on a fixed scope and lose badly on a growing one.
3. **Provisional sums and PC rates** are placeholders, not prices. Strip them out of the
   ranked total and compare the firm portion. They re-enter as risk.
4. **Measured vs lump sum.** A lump sum transfers quantity risk to the contractor and is
   worth a premium. Comparing a lump sum to a measured total ignores who carries the
   variation, which is usually the more expensive question.

---

## Unbalanced bidding — test it every time

The classic BOQ play: underprice lines likely to shrink, overprice lines likely to grow.
Total looks competitive at tender, exposure appears at final account.

**Test:** re-run the total with each of the top 10 lines varied ±20% in quantity. If the
ranking flips under plausible variation, the lowest tender is not the lowest cost, and that
is the headline finding, not a footnote.

Also check rate outliers directly: flag any rate more than 35% from the cross-vendor mean
on an identically-specified item. A sheet with several outliers in both directions and a
competitive total is the signature.

---

## Checks specific to this domain

1. Preliminaries as a % of works value, compared across vendors and against the norm the
   user supplies.
2. Whether rates are inclusive of material, labour, plant, wastage, scaffold, and how each
   vendor states it.
3. Wastage and lap allowances — buried in rate build-ups and a common quiet differentiator.
4. Escalation clause on a long programme. On a multi-year job, escalation treatment can
   exceed the entire tender gap.
5. Retention %, defect liability period, performance bond, LD rate and cap.
6. Site establishment, access, storage, working-hours restrictions — who carries them.
7. Statutory approvals, testing and commissioning, third-party inspection.
8. Attendance and coordination with other contractors, where multiple packages exist.
9. Programme: a shorter programme carries preliminaries savings but acceleration cost.
   Check the durations are credible before crediting the saving.
10. Measurement standard used, where the market has more than one.

---

## Reporting note

Report by **trade or section** as well as by line. A vendor who is cheap on civil and dear
on MEP is a packaging opportunity, and split award is often the correct recommendation —
provided you price the coordination burden it creates and say who carries the interfaces.
