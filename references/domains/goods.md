# Domain: goods, equipment, materials, manufacturing

Load when quotes are for physical products, plant, machinery, components or materials.

---

## What differs here

1. **Landed cost is the only honest number.** Ex-works, FOB, CIF and DDP are different
   products at different prices. Normalize every quote to **landed at point of use**,
   including freight, insurance, duty, clearing, inland transport, handling and unloading.
2. **The purchase price is a fraction of the cost.** For equipment with a service life,
   consumables, spares, energy, maintenance and downtime over the life typically exceed the
   capital cost. Compare over the life where the user can supply usage.
3. **Specification equivalence is rarely equivalence.** "Or equivalent" is doing enormous
   work in most quotes. Where a vendor offers an alternative make, that is a different
   product until proven otherwise, and should be evaluated as one.
4. **MOQ and batch pricing.** A rate at 10,000 units is not available at 1,200. Confirm the
   rate at the real quantity, in writing.

---

## Checks specific to this domain

1. **Incoterm** stated for each quote, and who carries risk at each stage.
2. **Duty and HSN classification** — a classification difference between vendors on the
   same goods is worth checking; it changes landed cost materially and one of them is wrong.
3. **Lead time and delivery schedule**, plus what happens on delay. A hard production date
   makes lead time a price component.
4. **Packing** — specification, cost, whether returnable, and who disposes of it.
5. **Inspection and acceptance** — pre-dispatch inspection, third-party certification,
   test certificates, rejection handling and who pays return freight.
6. **Warranty** — duration, from dispatch or from commissioning (a meaningful difference on
   long lead times), parts vs labour, on-site vs return-to-base.
7. **Spares** — availability, price list, lead time, and whether a recommended spares
   holding is required. Ask for a 2-year spares cost; it is where the margin often hides.
8. **Consumables lock-in** — proprietary consumables convert a capital decision into an
   annuity. Model it over the life.
9. **Energy and utility consumption** — rated draw against duty cycle. On continuously
   operating plant this can outweigh the entire purchase price gap within two years.
10. **Installed footprint and civil works** — foundations, power, cooling, access. Often
    excluded from every quote and payable by the buyer regardless.
11. **Training and documentation** — manuals, operator training, language.
12. **Compliance** — CE/BIS/UL or local certification. A non-compliant cheaper unit is not
    a cheaper option.
13. **Currency and FX exposure** on imported goods, including who bears movement between
    order and shipment.
14. **Payment security** — LC costs, advance exposure, and the counterparty risk that goes
    with an unknown overseas supplier.

---

## Reporting note

Show the **headline → landed → life-cycle** cascade as three columns side by side. The
reordering of vendors between those three columns is usually the entire finding, and it
lands harder as a visible reshuffle than as a paragraph.
